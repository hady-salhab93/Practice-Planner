# Establish the application's purpose and planning destination

## Question

What problem should Practice Planner solve, and what should this initial discovery effort establish before individual feature planning begins?

## Resolution

Captured from the user's completed purpose discussion on 2026-10-11, not a newly conducted feature interview.

- The user has many guitar materials in mind and lacks visibility into what receives attention and whether practice covers the intended material.
- The application should support a limited commitment for a period, followed by review, refinement, and another round of focus. Scrum provides inspiration rather than a mandatory full methodology.
- Breaking a difficult song, such as Dream Theater's "Take the Time," into manageable parts makes intentional practice more actionable.
- Musical improvement is judged by the user, not the application. Time records show practice attention, not proof of quality or efficiency.
- Use the name Sprint, default to fourteen days, and allow another duration when planning.
- Allow refinement during a Sprint and make changes visible during review. The exact change rules remain for later discovery.
- Use elapsed practice timing with explicit pause/resume rather than requiring a countdown or prescribed time targets.
- Review should expose total time by material, practice across days, and committed material receiving no attention. Display and calculation details remain unspecified.
- The current destination includes a shared application understanding, feature boundaries, a provisional first-version boundary, and a decision map. Individual feature grilling follows, rather than being replaced by this conversation.
- A complete selection-to-timing-to-review loop is the candidate first milestone; the capabilities necessary to make it usable are still to be decided.
